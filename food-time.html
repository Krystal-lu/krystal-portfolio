05 / Food Time / Immersive experience concept / 2025

Explore Hemudu food culture
through a shared meal.

What if visitors could learn about Hemudu food culture by joining a meal? Food Time proposes a table where picking up an object opens a story about fishing, keeping animals or growing rice on the surrounding screens and tabletop.

My contributionLoRA model training · Scene modeling & rendering · Character illustration · Animation

01 / Background & narrative

Food as a way
to encounter time.

Food Time imagines a spring feast inspired by Hemudu culture in Zhejiang. Food, pottery and seasonal rituals give visitors a way into the stories behind the meal.

Visitors sit at the table with illustrated Hemudu figures. The scenes follow food from the water, fields and animal enclosures back to a shared meal.

Fire / Food and cooperation

Keeping animals and cooking bring the meal back to work shared by the community.

Fields / Rice and craft

Rice and cooking vessels connect the food to the tools used to grow and prepare it.

Water / Fishing and ritual

Fishing brings the river into the story of the meal.

A shared spring feast

Each story returns to the shared table.

The team created this spring-feast story; it is not a reconstruction of a documented historical event.

02 / Character design

Character Design:
Ancient Hemudu People

I drew the figures with side profiles, layered clothing, ornaments and warm textures. Keeping the characters consistent helps the scenes feel like parts of the same world.

Hemudu character designs.

03 / Spatial & scene design

Experience Setup

Three screens surround the table, while a tabletop projection brings the animation closer to the visitor’s hands. The proposed setting uses a wooden table, stump seats, a woven mat, ceramic models and food objects.

Screen span4.8 m
Table footprint2.9 × 2.9 m
Screen height2.7 m
Table height0.8 m

Materials: three screens with aluminium frames, a natural-wood table and stump seats, a woven floor mat, ceramic models and food props.

Mountains, rice fields, water and animal enclosures form one landscape. The shared feast is the starting scene between object choices.

Concept film

Dining with the
Ancient Hemudu People

The film shows the proposed experience. Sit down and pick up one object: fish opens fishing, pork opens animal keeping, and rice opens cultivation. The screen scene and tabletop animation change together. Visitors can try the objects in any order.

I trained the LoRA model, modeled and rendered scenes, drew characters and made animations for this concept film. Object-triggered playback is proposed, not implemented.

05 / Interaction design

Choose fish, pork or rice, one object at a time. Each choice would change the screen scene and tabletop animation; there is no required order.

06 / Visual origins / Hemudu engraved art

From engraved motifs
to a living tabletop.

The tabletop illustrations draw on Hemudu engravings: pigs, rice and fish connect to food, while rhinoceroses and deer extend the surrounding animal world. The team turned these patterns into colored illustrations and animated responses.

07 / Tabletop interaction design

The story responds
at the scale of your hands.

Five motifs recur on the table: pigs, rice, fish, rhinoceroses and deer. They connect the projected illustrations with the pottery and animal-shaped objects visitors would handle.

Outcome & next evaluation

What we made, and what needs building.

Completed work

Proposed behavior

The pressure triggers and interactive projection have not been built.

Next, I would build a small object-triggered demo and watch whether visitors know where to sit, what to pick up and whether their action changed the scene. Timing, trigger reliability and resetting would need testing in the space.
