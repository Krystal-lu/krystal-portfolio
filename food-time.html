05 / Food Time / Immersive experience concept / 2025

A seat at the table.
A journey through time.

Food Time is an installation concept for experiencing the food and dining culture of ancient Hemudu. Visitors sit among ceramic vessels and animal-shaped objects, then pick up different objects to explore fishing, animal keeping and rice cultivation. Each choice changes both the scene in front of them and the animation on the tabletop.

01 / Background & narrative

Food as a way
to encounter time.

“Food is more than nourishment—it is a way of chewing over time.” This idea anchors Food Time’s imagined spring feast. The project draws on Hemudu culture in Zhejiang and brings food, vessels and seasonal rituals into one immersive setting.

The visitor joins the feast as a participant, sharing a table with stylized figures from the team’s narrative world. Fire, fields and water connect the stories of preparing food, cultivating rice and fishing. The spring banquet frames these activities as a collective welcome to a new agricultural season.

Fire / Food and cooperation

Animal keeping and cooking connect the meal to shared work and community life.

Fields / Rice and craft

Harvesting, cooking vessels and grain connect sustenance with tools and making.

Water / Fishing and ritual

The river supplies food and becomes part of the feast’s narrative of gratitude.

A shared spring feast

The three strands return to people gathering at the table.

The spring feast is the team’s designed narrative interpretation, rather than a claim to reconstruct a documented historical event.

03 / Spatial & scene design

Experience Setup

Three surrounding screens place the visitor inside the story while a projected tabletop provides the close-up interaction surface. The proposed installation combines a wood table and stump seats with a woven floor mat, ceramic models and food props.

Screen span4.8 m
Table footprint2.9 × 2.9 m
Screen height2.7 m
Table height0.8 m

Materials: three screens with aluminium frames, a natural-wood table and stump seats, a woven floor mat, ceramic models and food props.

The scenic composition connects a warm mountain landscape, rice fields, water and animal enclosures. These settings support the three food stories, with the shared feast serving as the standby scene.

Concept film

Dining with the
Ancient Hemudu People

The film presents the proposed cultural experience. A visitor takes a seat at the Hemudu dining table and picks up different objects: fish connects to fishing, pork to animal keeping, and rice to cultivation. Visitors choose one object at a time and can try the others at different moments, in any order. Each choice activates two coordinated responses—a living scene on the surrounding screens and a matching animation on the table. The vessels and animal motifs connect these actions to Hemudu food, drinking and everyday life.

Concept visualization · I created visual content through LoRA training, scene modeling and rendering, character illustration and animation. The film presents the proposed object-triggered experience.

05 / Interaction design

Choose an object.
Explore its story.

Take a seat and choose one of three food objects: fish, pork or rice. Pick up one object at a time to activate its corresponding scene and tabletop animation. Visitors can pick up the other objects at different moments to explore all three stories, in any order and at their own pace.

03 / Leave the seat

Both the surrounding screens and tabletop return to the standby composition.

This describes the designed interaction logic. Pressure-sensor triggering and interactive projection were not implemented.

06 / Visual origins / Hemudu engraved art

From engraved motifs
to a living tabletop.

The tabletop design takes its visual elements from the Hemudu references shown in the original project. Pig, rice and fish patterns connect the illustration to food and cultivation; rhinoceros and deer patterns extend the world of animals around the table. The team reinterprets these engraved forms as colored illustrations, then animates selected elements in response to the visitor’s object choice.

07 / Tabletop interaction design

The story responds
at the scale of your hands.

The tabletop illustration translates the project’s Hemudu engraved-art references into five recurring motifs: pigs, rice, fish, rhinoceroses and deer. These forms connect the projected surface to the vessels and animal objects within the experience. The physical ceramic vessels and animal-shaped objects evoke the dining and drinking culture represented in the installation.
