04 / Fitsole / Adaptive footwear / 2025

Different activities.
Different support needs.

Fixed arch support leaves little room to adjust between sitting, walking and running. Fitsole explores an inflatable support module and activity-based phone controls; the pressure sensor and pump worked, while the app connection remains a proposal.

Design focusProduct Design · User Experience · Interaction · Physical Prototyping
ContextIndividual project · June 2025
EvidenceOriginal concept, physical model, circuit and control logic

Design question

How could support adapt
without constant adjustment?

I focused on people with flat feet and the limits of fixed arch support. Could one shoe offer different support for different activities without needing constant adjustment?

The proposed service

From a foot scan to everyday use.

The proposed service runs from foot assessment and customization to everyday use and replacement. The wearer chooses the appearance; the support is intended to adjust during use.

  1. Assess & scan
  2. Customize
  3. Receive
  4. Use & adapt
  5. Renew or refit

01 / Assess & scan

Start with the foot, not a standard shoe size.

The service would begin with a flat-foot assessment and a 3D foot scan. The scan would guide the upper’s fit and the starting support shape.

A reusable fit record

Keep the scan so it can be reused or compared with a later assessment.

02 / Personalize

Choose the appearance. Keep the fit personal.

Using the foot record, the wearer would choose an upper shape, material and color, then confirm the combination.

Upper shapeMaterialColorConfirm design

03 / Receive

A customized shoe, delivered to the wearer.

The selected shoe would be made and delivered. The foot record would stay available for later changes.

04 / Use & adapt

Choose a mode. Let support respond.

The proposed app offers Sitting, Walking and Running modes. The broader concept also includes automatic adjustment as activity changes.

Pressure sensing and pump inflation worked in the physical prototype. Activity recognition and phone-controlled mode switching were not connected.

See the three modes and mobile UI ↓

05 / Renew or refit

Change what needs changing.

Foot shape unchanged

A new look, with the same support base.

If the fit is still suitable, the wearer could change the outer parts while keeping the support system and sole.

Foot shape or arch changes

Rescan, compare and customize again.

If a later scan showed a change in foot shape, it could guide replacement parts. This refitting service has not been tested.

Assessment, ordering, delivery and renewal are parts of the proposed service, not a service that has been launched.

Two ways to adapt

Sense the load.
Let the wearer choose the context.

Pressure-responsive support

The physical prototype reads pressure in three ranges and uses a relay to run the pump for different lengths of time. It inflated successfully, but I did not achieve precise support adjustment.

User-selected activity

The proposed mobile flow lets the wearer choose Running, Walking or Sitting from the overview.

Modular product system

Keep the support.
Renew the outer layer.

Replaceable outer layer

The upper could be customized for fit and appearance, then replaced without discarding the whole shoe when the fit remains suitable.

Arch-support air module

An internal air module changes shape as it inflates.

Retained frame and sole

The frame and sole hold the support mechanism. The concept keeps these parts when only the upper needs replacing.

From concept to prototype

A physical model.
A concrete response rule.

01 / SenseRead a pressure value.
02 / ClassifyAssign low, medium or high range.
03 / ActivateSwitch the relay and pump.
04 / RespondUse a 2, 4 or 6-second inflation period.
05 / RecheckThe documented logic restarts when pressure increases.

The prototype uses three pressure ranges to run the pump for 2, 4 or 6 seconds.

What I would test next

Make the experience
predictable and understandable.

Next, I would test comfort over time, whether the current mode is clear, how a wearer stops an adjustment, and what happens if a sensor or pump fails.

The pressure sensor and pump worked. Precise support control still needs development, and the mobile interface remains a separate concept.
