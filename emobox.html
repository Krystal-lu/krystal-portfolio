03 / EmoBox / Connected product & interaction design / 2025

A companion you can turn to
when you want to.

A check-in can feel like another demand when it arrives at the wrong moment. EmoBox gives people a small invitation to engage, then lets them try an activity, draw another card or stop.

The opportunity

Support should not become
another demand.

EmoBox is for young people living alone who may find it hard to talk about their feelings. The challenge was to offer company without making them feel they had to respond.

How could the companion offer support without deciding what someone should do next?

Testing & iteration

Let people decide when to start and stop.

The test notes pointed to two problems: a prompt could interrupt someone at a bad moment, and drawing cards could feel too restrictive. Both made the choice to start or stop important.

Start quietly.

One person said a phone pop-up made a bad mood feel worse. The test notes suggested a small vibration from Momo instead.

A vibration could be easier to miss, but it would also be less demanding. I proposed it as a first signal, leaving the phone check-in for when the person wanted to open it.

Let “no” be a complete response.

Another comment asked for more freedom to skip a card or stop drawing. An activity meant to help should not feel like something to finish.

The later flow includes options to try, redraw or leave. People can also open Daily mode themselves instead of waiting for a prompt from Momo.

These changes respond to the feedback, but their effect has not been measured. I would next test whether people can decline or leave easily and whether the quieter prompt feels less intrusive.

Two ways to connect

Follow a prompt or open the app yourself.

People can enter in two ways: follow a signal from Momo, or open Daily mode to draw a card whenever they feel like it.

01 / Emotion-triggered mode

Notice → personalize → choose → reflect

An expression cue leads to an AI-generated emotion card. From there, the person can draw an activity, talk with the card’s spirit or look back at their recorded feelings.

02 / Daily mode

Open → draw → enjoy

Open the app, draw a card and decide whether to try it. No expression cue is needed.

Emotion-triggered / 01

Detect a feeling.
Invite a check-in.

The original prototype used an expression cue to trigger a phone notification. After the feedback, I proposed a light vibration from Momo as the first invitation. The screens below still show the original notification flow.

The person can open the notification or leave it for later. Opening it leads to a personalized emotion card.

Emotion-triggered / 02

An emotion card
made for this moment.

AI generates an illustrated card from the detected expression cue. The scene and its small spirit give the person something to respond to beyond an emotion label.

The card designs include happiness, loneliness and feeling speechless. Each leads into the same check-in and activity flow.

Emotion-triggered / 03

Draw a card.
Choose your next step.

Pick a face-down card and open it to reveal a small activity suggestion.

Try the activity, decline it, draw another card or leave. The person can also talk with the spirit and give feedback.

Emotion-triggered / 04

Talk to the little spirit.
Tell it what helped.

After drawing a card, the person can talk with its spirit using voice, sharing what is on their mind or responding to the activity.

The feedback screen asks how the experience felt and what went wrong, such as an inaccurate reply, slow response or repeated content.

Emotion-triggered / 05

See feelings accumulate
over time.

The report brings earlier check-ins together, so people can look back at more than their most recent entry.

Activity feedback records what helped in one session. The report lets people review their entries over time.

Daily mode

No trigger needed.
Just a little surprise.

Daily mode is for opening the app without waiting for a signal from Momo.

Draw a card, read the suggestion and decide what to do. Redraw and exit remain available.
